Iron Deficiency Anemia (IDA)
Microcytic Hypochromic

The most prevalent anemia globally, caused by depleted iron stores. Red blood cells display reduced hemoglobin content, expanded central pallor, and reduced MCV (< 80 fL).

Key Smear Markers:
Microcytes, hypochromasia, pencil/cigar cells, anisocytosis.
Vitamin B12 Deficiency
Macrocytic Megaloblastic

Impaired erythrocyte maturation due to defective DNA synthesis. Marked by abnormally large erythrocytes (MCV > 100 fL) and hypersegmented neutrophils.

Key Smear Markers:
Oval macrocytes, hypersegmented polymorphs (≥5 lobes), Howell-Jolly bodies.
Sickle Cell Anemia (SCA)
Hemoglobinopathy (HbS)

A hereditary disorder where point mutation in the beta-globin chain causes hemoglobin S polymerization, deforming cells into rigid crescent or sickle shapes.

Key Smear Markers:
Sickle cells (drepanocytes), target cells, boat-shaped cells, polychromasia.
Thalassemia
Genetic Hemoglobin Defect

Genetic reduction in alpha or beta globin synthesis. Characterized by disproportionately severe microcytosis relative to high RBC count (Mentzer Index < 13).

Key Smear Markers:
Target cells (codocytes), marked microcytosis, basophilic stippling, teardrop cells.
Dimorphic Anemia
Dual Erythrocyte Population

Simultaneous coexistence of two distinct populations of red blood cells. Most commonly observed in combined iron and B12/folate deficiencies or post-transfusion.

Key Smear Markers:
Two distinct RBC populations: hypochromic microcytes and normo/macrocytes.
